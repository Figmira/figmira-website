## The day they said no

April 30, 2002. Hynix Semiconductor is barely alive. Memory prices have crashed, the banks it owes money to have taken control, and it's carrying about $6.5 billion of debt.

On the table sits a lifeline from its American rival, Micron: Micron stock worth about $3.5 billion, plus a $200 million investment, in exchange for Hynix's memory business. Taking it would make Micron the biggest memory maker in the world. It would also end Hynix.

The board votes. Unanimously, it says no. The CEO resigns ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/hynix-board-kills-deal-with-micron/)). One headline at the time put it bluntly: "Hynix Hurts Itself by Rejecting Micron Offer" ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/hynix-hurts-itself-by-rejecting-micron-offer-10020187)).

Twenty-four years later, that company, now called SK hynix, makes the memory that sits beside the most powerful AI chips on Earth. In just the last three months it reported, it earned $43.6 billion in operating profit. More than twelve times what Micron once offered for its memory business, earned in a single quarter.

That stubborn "no" is where our story starts. Since July 2026, anyone in the U.S. can own a piece of it through SKHY, its new [Nasdaq shares](https://news.skhynix.com/en/skhynix-lists-adrs-on-nasdaq/). And we believe SKHY can run to **$780 a share by 2032**, about four times where it trades today.

Here's our case.

## The bottleneck is remembering

Every AI model does two things: it thinks, and it remembers. The thinking happens in processors like Nvidia's. The remembering happens in memory chips, and that's where SK hynix lives.

There are two main kinds. **DRAM** is short-term memory: fast, and it forgets when the power goes off. **NAND** is long-term storage: slower, but it remembers. The most important kind today is **HBM**, or high-bandwidth memory: DRAM chips stacked like a tiny skyscraper and placed right beside an AI processor so data can move at enormous speed.

An AI chip without enough fast memory is like a genius who can only read one word at a time. The smarter the models get, the more memory they need. And AI is leaving the screen. Every robot that sees, moves, and learns will need memory of its own.

So, what if AI's biggest bottleneck isn't thinking, but remembering? We think it already is. Look at where the money is going:

Out of every $100 of chips it sold, $76 was left over as operating profit. For a business that runs giant factories, that is almost unheard of. It also holds far more cash ($63.4 billion) than debt ($13.4 billion), so it can keep building no matter what the market does.

And it's building. About 10 major customers have signed long-term supply deals with SK hynix. Its newest AI memory, HBM4, started shipping this year. The M15X factory is speeding up, and a new cleanroom at its Yongin site opens in early 2027 ([SK hynix](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/)). Companies don't build new factories for demand they think is about to disappear.
