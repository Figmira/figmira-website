Quick question before you read on: if you started something tomorrow, what are the odds it works? Out of 10.

Hold that number. We'll come back to it.

## The odds were never the point

In 1988, researchers Arnold Cooper, Carolyn Woo, and William Dunkelberg asked 2,994 people who had just started businesses that same question. [Most of them rated their odds as very high.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0883902688900201)

Four out of five founders believe they're headed for success. Within ten years, nearly two out of three businesses are gone. By any sober math, most of those founders were wrong.

And yet Daniel Kahneman, who won a Nobel Prize for studying how human judgment goes wrong, titled his chapter on this exact bias ["The Engine of Capitalism."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow) His point was that optimists take the risks everyone else avoids. Many of them lose. But the world moves forward because they tried.

Jensen Huang built Nvidia into one of the most valuable companies on Earth. When the *Acquired* podcast asked him in 2023 whether he would start a company again, [he said he wouldn't](https://fortune.com/2023/10/23/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-tech-startup-entrepreneur). Building Nvidia, he explained, turned out to be "a million times harder than I expected it to be."

That's the delusion that matters: either not knowing how hard it will be, or knowing and going anyway.

When the future is this uncertain, most people wait for proof, for permission, or for someone else to go first. That's exactly why the people who move now have room to run. The frontier is widest when everyone else is standing still.

## Delusional about the destination, disciplined about the road

Blind optimism has a real cost. Kahneman and Dan Lovallo warned in *Harvard Business Review* that [optimism leads executives to overestimate benefits and underestimate costs](https://hbr.org/2003/07/delusions-of-success-how-optimism-undermines-executives-decisions). Unchecked, it burns money, time, and people.

The answer isn't less optimism. It's putting the optimism in the right place. Jim Collins calls this [the Stockdale Paradox](https://www.jimcollins.com/concepts/Stockdale-Concept.html): keep unwavering faith that you'll prevail in the end, while facing the most brutal facts about where you stand today.

SpaceX's first rocket shows it best. [Falcon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_1) failed in 2006. It failed again in 2007. It failed a third time in August 2008. On September 28, 2008, the fourth launch reached orbit, the first privately developed liquid-fueled rocket ever to do so. The belief never changed. The engineering changed every single time.

That's the kind of delusional I mean:

<blockquote class="fx-pull"><p>Impossible about the dream. Ruthless about the facts.</p></blockquote>

## Optimism keeps you in the game

There's one more reason to stay optimistic, and it has nothing to do with business. A 2019 study in *PNAS* followed about 70,000 women for 10 years and about 1,400 men for 30 years, and compared the most optimistic people with the least.

The study shows a link, not proof that optimism causes a longer life. But the pattern matches what every builder learns the hard way: the people who believe it will work are the ones still standing when it finally does.

## Why I started Figmira

I'm not an engineer by training. I'm new to code. I built this website anyway, one piece at a time, with AI as my co-pilot, breaking things and fixing them until it felt like a door into another world.

By sober math, I had no business doing it. That's exactly why Figmira exists: to study the technologies reshaping the world, like AI, blockchain, robotics, and markets, and to build with imagination first, even when the odds say wait.

## How Figmira practices delusional optimism

- **Dream at full size.** We don't shrink ideas to fit today's odds. Every world starts as a figment.
- **Research like a skeptic.** These notes exist to face the brutal facts about AI, blockchain, robotics, and markets, including the ones we don't like.
- **Keep launching.** Falcon 1 needed four tries. We'll probably need more. Each attempt is data, not a verdict.
- **Protect the light.** When the news is loud and the mood is nervous, keep the ✦ lit.
